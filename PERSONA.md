# PERSONA.md - Keyboard Drummer

## The person

**"Dez", 24, bedroom producer and part-time barista in Austin.** Makes lo-fi and boom-bap beats in FL Studio at night on a 2021 M1 MacBook Air with a 61-key MIDI controller he barely uses for drums (he draws hats in the piano roll instead, and feels vaguely guilty about it). Played 2 years of drumline in high school; still air-drums at red lights. He is not a "gamer" per se, but he has 900 hours in osu! muscle memory from age 16 and gets competitive fast when a score screen appears.

This is a deliberate refinement of the V2 evangelist (20-28 bedroom producer / music student), not an overrule. The V2 read was right; the sharpening is that the person who actually screenshots and posts is the **producer with latent rhythm-game history**, not the pure music student. The music student compares this to a practice pad and finds it lacking; the ex-osu!/DDR/Clone Hero producer compares it to a rhythm game and finds it charming and free. Optimize for the second person.

## The exact job he hires this for

"I have 4 minutes between renders / while a sample pack downloads, and I want to hit drums with my hands RIGHT NOW without opening my DAW, plugging in a controller, or installing anything." Secondary job: proving to the group chat that his drumline hands still work, in screenshot form.

- **r/rhythmgames** and the **Clone Hero Discord** (#clips channel is exactly where a combo GIF of this would land)

## What he uses today instead

- FL Studio piano roll / FPC pads (the "real" tool, high friction for a 4-minute itch)
- **Drumbit** or **Splice beat maker** browser toys (step sequencers, not performance - they don't test his hands)
- **Clone Hero** / **osu!** (performance, but a whole install-and-launch commitment)
- Idle tapping on his desk (the true competitor)

## 3 NAMED reference products he considers premium

1. **Clone Hero** - the taste benchmark for "falling notes done right": readable lanes, juicy combo feedback, score screen worth posting.
2. **Ableton's Learning Music (learningmusic.ableton.com)** - the benchmark for "a music toy in a browser can feel expensive": instant sound, zero chrome, beautiful restraint.
3. **Teenage Engineering (OP-1 / EP-133 K.O. II)** - the hardware aesthetic he screenshots and can't afford; industrial playfulness, labeled keys, everything feels like an instrument panel.

## His vocabulary

Says "pocket", "groove", "quantized", "FC" (full combo), "clean run", "hands", "cook / cooked", "locked in", "no misses". Grades matter to him as letters: an "S rank" is a flex, "98% accuracy" is a receipt. He would never say "gamify" or "engagement". Calls the product category "a little browser rhythm game" affectionately, not dismissively.

## His device

M1 MacBook Air, Chrome, half the time with AirPods, often at 80% battery in a coffee shop. Physical keyboard is a hard requirement he understands instinctively (the mobile gate does not offend him if it is honest and fast).

## 5-second bounce triggers

1. **First keypress makes no sound** (audio-unlock friction or focus lost) - the fatal one; he assumes it is broken and closes the tab.
2. **Cannot tell which keys do what before starting** - if he has to open a help accordion to play, he is gone.
3. Layout reads like a dashboard/SaaS template instead of an instrument or arcade cabinet.
4. Input latency he can feel - anything spongy between key and drum sound and his drummer brain rejects it.
5. A wall of settings before the first hit. He wants: click song, count-in, playing, inside 10 seconds.

## The unprompted-share moment

He finishes a hard song with a long max combo and the results screen shows a big letter grade, accuracy %, max combo, and the streak flame - composed well enough that the screenshot IS the flex. He posts it to the Clone Hero Discord #clips or quote-posts on X with "drumline hands never die". The share button must pre-write that tweet for him (grade + accuracy + song + link) so the lazy path and the flex path are the same path. Bonus share trigger: the mid-run "ON FIRE" 24+ streak state filling the screen - that is the GIF moment.
